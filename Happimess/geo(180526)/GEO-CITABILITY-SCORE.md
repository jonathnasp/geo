## Scoring Methodology

Each passage is scored on 5 dimensions (0–100 each):

| Dimension | Weight | What It Measures |
|-----------|--------|-----------------|
| Answer Block Quality | 25% | Does it directly answer a likely AI query? |
| Self-Containment | 20% | Understandable without surrounding context? |
| Structural Readability | 20% | Headers, bullets, tables — scannable format? |
| Statistical Density | 20% | Specific numbers, measurements, durations? |
| Uniqueness | 15% | Proprietary/original vs. generic industry knowledge? |

## Site-Wide Citability Gaps

### Gap 1: Zero External Citations (Critical)
Every blog article analyzed — zero outbound links to external sources. This is the single largest citability suppressor. AI systems use external citations as a quality signal: a page that cites authoritative sources is treated as more reliable than one that doesn't.

**Fix:** Add 2–3 external citations per article. For trash management content: EPA solid waste data, ANSI container sizing standards. For scented products: consumer fragrance research. Target one government source and one recognized media source per article.

**Estimated citability impact:** +8–12 points on affected articles.

---

### Gap 2: Anonymous Authorship (High)
All content uses "From The Mess Experts" or "Happimess editorial team" — never a named individual with credentials. AI systems assign lower weight to anonymously authored content when multiple candidate pages exist for the same query.

**Fix:** Add a named author byline to every article. Minimum viable: "Written by [Name], Home Organization Specialist at Happimess." Full implementation: an `/pages/meet-our-authors` page with bios linking to article archives.

**Estimated citability impact:** +5–8 points on affected articles.

---

### Gap 3: Proprietary Data Not Deployed (High)
The About page contains unique, specific, and credible research data (30-day testing protocol, 500+ cycle test, 15-day odor test, compatibility testing with Glad/Hefty/Simplehuman). None of this appears in any blog article. This is the site's most significant missed opportunity.

**Fix:** Add a standard callout block to every product guide article: "This recommendation reflects Happimess's 30-day evaluation protocol, which includes 500+ mechanism cycles and a 15-day odor containment test." This directly elevates uniqueness and answer block quality scores on all product articles.

**Estimated citability impact:** +6–10 points on affected articles.

---

### Gap 4: No Statistics in Blog Content (Medium)
Current state: all product claims are qualitative assertions ("better odor control," "stronger drawstrings," "more durable"). No third-party statistics, no measurement data, no comparison benchmarks.

**Fix per article:**
- Dual Trash Can: cite ANSI container capacity standards or EPA kitchen waste averages
- Trash Bag: cite a consumer research figure on household odor complaints or bag failure rates
- Home Decor: cite a home organization survey or industry spending statistic

**Estimated citability impact:** +4–6 points per article.

---

### Gap 5: No Structured Callouts for Key Claims (Medium)
The best passages (testing protocol, capacity tiers, recommendations) are embedded in prose paragraphs. AI models extract from structured elements more reliably than from continuous prose.

**Fix:** Use `<blockquote>`, `<table>`, or callout `<div>` elements to surface key claims. The capacity tiers passage (30–40L / 40–60L / 60L+) is already partially structured — ensure it renders as a visual table or list in the Shopify theme.
