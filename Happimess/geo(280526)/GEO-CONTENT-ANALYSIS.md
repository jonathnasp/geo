## Priority Actions

### 1. [CRITICAL] Rename or replace AI-generated image files before next crawl

The filenames `ChatGPT_Image_May_4_2026_11_30_24_AM.png` and `ChatGPT_Image_May_4_2026_11_30_22_AM.png` are visible in the HTML source of the trash bag article. AI search engines parse image metadata. These filenames signal low-quality AI content generation to both human reviewers and automated systems.

Action: In Shopify Admin → Files, rename or re-upload the images with descriptive, keyword-relevant filenames (e.g., `happimess-lemon-scented-trash-bag-liner.jpg`, `happimess-lavender-drawstring-trash-liner.jpg`). Update the image src references in the article.

### 2. [CRITICAL] Audit and correct all government citation paraphrases

The EPA, USDA, and CDC citations in the trash bag article are paraphrased sentences that do not appear in the linked source materials. Example: "food waste is one of the largest contributors to household trash odor and should be disposed of properly to help maintain cleaner kitchen environments" is attributed to the EPA but is not an EPA quote.

Action: For each government citation, open the linked page and either (a) find a verbatim quote that supports the claim and use it with quotation marks, or (b) remove the citation if no supporting text exists. Replace fabricated statistics with documented facts. A false citation attributed to a federal agency is a trustworthiness failure that AI models — which cross-reference authoritative sources — will penalize.

### 3. [CRITICAL] Add verifiable credentials to author bios

The Meet Our Authors page lists Jonathan Yaraghi as "Home Organization Expert" with a generic passion-statement bio. No evidence of expertise is present: no education, no certifications, no years of experience, no publications, no industry affiliations.

Action: Add Jonathan Y brand history and Safavieh connection to Jonathan Yaraghi's bio. Add specific: years of experience, notable projects or clients, any media appearances, or industry affiliations. Add headshots. Add LinkedIn profile links for both authors. Connect named authors to their individual articles in the article byline (replace "From The Mess Experts" with the author's name and a link to their bio page).

### 4. [HIGH] Standardize author attribution across all articles

Four articles use three different attribution strings: "Happimess Editorial Team," "From The Mess Experts," and (implied) the Meet Our Authors page authors. No article byline connects to a named author.

Action: Replace all article bylines with the named author format: "By Jonathan Yaraghi, Home Organization Expert" with a link to /pages/meet-our-authors. Update the BlogPosting schema `author` field to match. This is required for the named-author schema already in place to have any E-E-A-T value.

### 5. [HIGH] Add external citations to Economy Home Decor article

This 3,500-word article contains zero external citations. All "external" links route to Happimess.com product pages. A guide claiming to advise on home decor trends, styling principles, and budget strategies with no third-party data is not credible to AI citation systems.

Action: Add 3–5 external citations from authoritative sources — Census Bureau data on housing trends, NAHB or Houzz market research on home decor spending, or academic research on the relationship between organized spaces and wellbeing. Integrate these as linked inline references, not a reference list at the end.

### 6. [HIGH] Add 4–6 knowledge-based Q&As to the FAQ page

The FAQ page contains 8 questions, all 100% transactional (shipping, returns, promo codes). This page contributes zero expertise signals to E-E-A-T and is ineligible for FAQ schema optimization.

Action: Add a second FAQ section titled "Product & Organization Questions" with entries such as:
- "What size trash can is right for my kitchen?" (with specific volume guidance)
- "What's the difference between step, sensor, and pedal trash cans?"
- "How often should I replace my kitchen trash can liner?"
- "Are your trash bags compatible with all can sizes?"
- "What does 'dual compartment' mean and do I need it?"

These questions are already answered informally in blog articles — the FAQ page should surface that expertise.

### 7. [MEDIUM] Publish blog listing dates on /blogs/news

The blog listing page shows article excerpts but no publication dates. Visitors and AI crawlers cannot assess content freshness from the listing view.

Action: In Shopify theme code, add `{{ article.published_at | date: "%B %d, %Y" }}` to the blog listing template. This is a low-effort Liquid template change with outsized freshness signaling benefit.

### 8. [MEDIUM] Add the founder narrative to About Us

The About Us page describes "product designers, home organization specialists, and customer service professionals" without naming anyone. The founder's background (Jonathan Y furniture brand, Safavieh sourcing experience) is the strongest available authority signal on the site and is completely absent.

Action: Add a founder section to /pages/about-us: name, background, what problem Happimess was founded to solve, specific experience that qualifies the brand to curate home organization products. This is the single highest-impact E-E-A-T addition available and requires no external validation.

### 9. [MEDIUM] Expand the dual trash can article to match its "Complete 2026 Guide" title

At ~1,200 words, this article is shorter than the average how-to post despite being titled a "complete guide." It also carries a "2026" freshness claim that creates an expectation of current data.

Action: Expand to 2,000–2,500 words by adding: a full comparison of competing products (not just Happimess), specific data on recycling contamination rates from EPA (actual verbatim statistics), a sizing calculator or decision tree, and real installation or setup notes from hands-on testing.

### 10. [LOW] Remove Economy Home Decor from primary topical cluster

This article targets "economy home decor" — a tangential keyword with no clear connection to Happimess's core products (trash cans, storage furniture, organization). The article has zero external citations and is the weakest piece in the audited set.

Action: Either (a) rewrite with a clear connection to Happimess products (storage ottomans, wicker trunks, organization furniture) and add 3–5 external citations, or (b) de-prioritize by removing from navigation and internal link structure, and redirect SEO effort to on-brand topics (trash can selection, kitchen organization, bathroom storage).

## Summary

The Happimess content architecture has the structural skeleton of an E-E-A-T-compliant blog: named authors, a dedicated author page, BlogPosting schema with dates, and external citation links. What it lacks is the substance behind those structures. The author bios are credential-free. The citations are paraphrased fabrications. The attribution is inconsistent. The images carry ChatGPT timestamps in their filenames. The FAQ is entirely transactional.

AI citation systems do not reward structural compliance — they reward demonstrated authority, verifiable facts, and content that answers questions better than alternatives. At present, Happimess blog content would be unlikely to be cited by Perplexity, ChatGPT, or Gemini on any topic where an authoritative competitor exists, despite ranking improvements those structural fixes may have delivered in traditional search.

The highest-ROI actions are the founder narrative (zero-cost, maximum authority impact) and citation correction (trust-critical, required before AI crawlers recrawl). The image filename rename is a simple hygiene fix that eliminates an active AI-content disclosure signal.
